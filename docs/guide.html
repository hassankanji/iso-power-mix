Reading the Generation Mix Tracker

What the numbers are, where they come from, and what not to read into them.

← Back to the dashboard

Daily electricity generation by fuel for all seven U.S. ISOs/RTOs, in one place, updated every morning. It exists to answer supply questions — above all how much gas is being burned, where, and against what — without opening seven different market websites.

The short version

  • Updated twice daily, by mid-morning ET, through yesterday for every market.
  • Nothing to install or log into. The page is static; the link works for anyone.
  • The numbers come from the markets themselves — CAISO, PJM, ERCOT, MISO, SPP, NYISO and ISO-NE each publish their own fuel mix — with the federal EIA-930 dataset used to fill gaps and to draw the national total.
  • If the data goes stale, the site says so in a banner at the top. No banner means the newest day is current.

The five tabs

National Trend
Everything the seven ISOs generated, stacked by fuel, day by day. The dashed line above the stack is EIA's measurement of the entire lower 48 — the gap between them is the third of the country that never joined an ISO (the utility-run Southeast, Northwest and Southwest). Tick % of total to read mix share instead of volume; that is where gas-for-coal displacement is easiest to see.
Fuel Comparison
One fuel at a time, one line per market, so you can see which market is driving a move. The dashed US lower-48 line is the true national number for that fuel — total U.S. gas burn, say — and it is far larger than any single ISO, so untick it in the legend when you want to compare markets against each other. % of each ISO's total normalizes market size away. Smoothing is a 7-day trailing average, on by default.
National by ISO
The same national total, split by market instead of by fuel. Answers "who is big" and "whose load is moving".
Per-ISO Breakdown
One market's full fuel mix over time.
Latest Snapshot
The most recent day in detail — see below.

Every chart has a Stacked / Lines switch (stacked answers "what was the total and who contributed"; lines answer "where is this one series going" without the series underneath moving the baseline), a checkbox legend for excluding series, range presets from 1D to Max, and free date pickers.

The Latest Snapshot tab, and the toggle worth knowing about

The snapshot shows the newest day at three levels — the whole lower 48, the seven tracked ISOs summed, then each market on its own — with each fuel's output, its share of that day, and how it has changed.

Two controls sit above it. Change vs picks the comparison period: yesterday, a week, a month, a year, or all four at once. Change shown as switches what the change columns measure:

Energy (GWh) — how many gigawatt-hours moved. Straightforward, but load moves every fuel at once: on a hot day every column is green, on a mild day every column is red, and neither tells you who gained ground.

Share of mix (pp) — how many percentage points of the mix each fuel gained or lost. Gas at 42.1% today against 39.8% a year ago reads +2.3 pp. This divides the load move out, so displacement shows up as one fuel moving against another. Hover any cell to see both underlying shares and the exact day compared.

Percentage points, not percent: +2.3 pp is the share moving from 39.8 to 42.1, not a 2.3% change in gas burn. The Total row keeps showing the load itself, in percent, since a total's share of itself is always 100%.

With All four selected in share mode you get the whole trajectory at a glance — day, week, month and year side by side, per fuel, per market.

Each market is compared against its own latest day shifted back, not a fixed calendar date, because the ISOs publish on different delays. A blank change cell means there is nothing to compare against — it never means zero.

What to trust, and what not to

Each of these is a real property of the underlying data, not a bug:

Battery is discharge only
Energy sent out of the battery fleet. Charging is excluded — it is load, not generation — so the battery number never goes below zero and is not the net battery position. Pumped hydro is not in this bucket; it sits in Hydro, where every ISO files it. On the Fuel Comparison tab the national battery line runs below CAISO's own line, because CAISO, PJM and NYISO report no battery series to EIA at all — that national figure is a floor, not a total.
CAISO's absolute level is not comparable to EIA's
CAISO's public feed includes imports (~13% of its total) that EIA's net generation figure excludes. Trends and mix are unaffected; the level runs about 20% above EIA's measurement of the same grid.
ISO-NE runs slightly low
Its API covers market-metered units; EIA also estimates small non-market resources, so ISO-NE reads about 6% below EIA.
The freshest ERCOT and MISO days are marked "preliminary"
They come from live telemetry and are replaced automatically by settlement-quality data — ERCOT's within one to four weeks. The label disappears on its own.
Smaller print
NYISO dual-fuel units count as gas, so severe winter oil-switching days overstate NYISO gas somewhat. SPP's day boundary is GMT, a few hours off the others. Anything a source reports that doesn't map to one of the nine buckets lands in "Imports / Other" rather than being dropped.

Everything here is checked against EIA-930's independent federal measurements — per fuel, not just on the total — and the five markets without a structural difference agree to within half a percent.

If something looks wrong

  • A stale-data banner at the top tells you how old the newest day is. Past 8 days it turns red: the daily update has probably stopped and the repo owner needs to know.
  • No banner but a number looks off: check the Data freshness & coverage table at the bottom of the Latest Snapshot tab — it shows each market's newest day and any unfilled gaps.
  • A sudden cliff or spike in one bucket is worth reporting. Most of the caveats above were found exactly that way.
